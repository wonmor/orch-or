The theory in sixty seconds

Orchestrated Objective Reduction is physicist Roger Penrose's and anaesthesiologist Stuart Hameroff's proposal that consciousness arises from quantum events inside the microtubules of neurons. Tubulin proteins are held to enter superpositions, become entangled across the lattice, and then collapse on their own through a gravitational mechanism Penrose calls objective reduction. Each collapse is a discrete moment of experience.

τ ≈ ħ / EG

The threshold is set by the gravitational self-energy of the difference between the superposed branches. Hameroff and Penrose calibrate it so that about 2×10¹⁰ tubulins self-collapse in 25 ms, matching 40 Hz gamma synchrony.

The main objection is decoherence: the brain is warm and wet, and Max Tegmark calculated that microtubule superpositions would die in about 10⁻¹³ s. Hagan, Hameroff and Tuszynski argued for 10⁻⁴ s with shielding. Both are far short of 25 ms. Proponents point to recent experiments on anaesthetics acting on microtubules and on collective optical effects in tubulin. The app presents all of this and lets you compute the numbers yourself.

Orch-OR is a minority view in neuroscience. The app is a teaching model of a contested theory, and says so.
